Why blue is not always neutral

A blue core can carry line or switched line when identified with brown at both ends. Colour alone does not decide its function. The earth core remains protective.

The lesson examples use supply and lamp connections at Switch 1, with three cable runs: supply to Switch 1, Switch 1 to lamp, and Switch 1 to Switch 2. Complete conventional two-way control here needs three working cores between the switches. Brown identification does not add the missing switching path. In the completed mechanical-switch example, all three link cores have line functions; Switch 2 has no neutral connection.

Connecting the return to N join is a separate change: the neutral-short example creates a line-to-neutral path in one switch combination. The live table and Circuit checks describe the current edited plan.

WELCOME TO THE WORKBENCH

Explore how a lighting circuit connects.

Build, connect, try

  1. Drag in a part. Move a consumer unit, lamp, switch or socket from the dock onto the board. You can also click a part to add it, then drag its header to arrange it.
  2. Start a cable at a terminal. Choose the cable type and core size in the dock. Drag from a terminal on one component to a terminal on another, then choose which physical core makes that connection.
  3. Connect the remaining cores. Select the cable to reveal its coloured endpoint handles. Drag each end to a terminal on that same component. Drop an end on blank board space to disconnect it; dropping it on a different component cancels the move.
  4. Use the controls when you prefer. The Connect button lets you choose two components. Select the cable and use the inspector’s terminal and identification controls to finish or change its connections.
  5. Try a lighting-only circuit. Flip a switch on the board and watch the lamp. Open Checks to review all switch combinations, circuit warnings and design estimates.
  6. Add design inputs when needed. Select a cable for its length and installation conditions. Select the consumer unit for earthing, device type and external loop impedance.

Keyboard: use Tab to focus a terminal or core end, then Enter or Space to start and finish a connection. Escape cancels. Native inspector controls remain available for every core.

Read the model

JN and JS are separate joining connectors inside a switch box. They are not part of the switch contacts. A two-way switch changes COM between L1 and L2. An intermediate switch changes two connections together: Straight joins A1–B1 and A2–B2; Crossed joins A1–B2 and A2–B1. A1, A2, B1 and B2 are this app’s model names, not universal product terminal markings.

Cable cores keep their physical colours. Identification sleeves can mark a core’s role at its ends. A blue core used for a line function needs the appropriate identification; the protective earth core must remain protective earth.

Lighting simulation supports one protected supply, one ideal Class I lamp, a nominal 230 V supply and up to six switches. Core size is a cross-sectional area in mm², separate from the number of cores in a cable.

Ceiling-rose LOOP

Select the lamp and enable Ceiling rose · include LOOP to add a separate permanent-line joining terminal. In this model, LOOP has no internal connection to the lamp’s L, N or PE terminals. L is the lamp’s switched-line input, N is neutral, and PE remains protective. Terminal markings and arrangements vary by product.

The Three switches + socket ring example leaves LOOP unused: its feed is at S1, and the lamp receives switched line, neutral and protective earth. Adding LOOP does not move the feed or change the sketch’s layout-only scope.

Control from three or more places

Choose Three-switch lighting to try two two-way switches with an intermediate switch between them. Each of the three switches should change the lamp, whatever the others’ positions. More intermediate switches can be added between the two end switches; this app supports up to four intermediates and six switches in total.

In this example, the brown switched-line return passes through the intermediate box’s separate Line join (JS) connector. It does not pass through an intermediate-switch contact. The black and grey traveller cores connect the switching pairs and have brown identification at both ends. Earth remains protective.

Socket sketches are layout only

Choose Three switches + socket ring for the requested arrangement: CCU to S1, S1 to the lamp, S1 to intermediate S2, and S2 to S3, alongside a separate socket route CCU → SK1 → SK2 → CCU.

Sockets have L, N and PE terminals. The consumer unit’s optional socket group has RL, RN and RPE; these are app labels for a separate group. The example uses 2.5 mm² twin-and-earth for the socket loop. Selecting that cable size does not establish its suitability for an installation.

Any plan containing a socket or the socket terminal group is layout only, including its lighting. Lamp operation, switch behavior, fault detection and design checks are not assessed for that plan. You can arrange parts, connect cores, and save or share the sketch, without a simulation or safety verdict.

Compare the blue-core lesson

A blue switched-line core with brown identification can be valid. The lesson separates missing identification, incomplete independent two-way control, and a line-to-neutral short. The examples keep supply and lamp at Switch 1; the completed link has three working cores plus protective earth.

Enable the switch-behavior goal to ask whether either switch changes the lamp in both positions of the other. The table shows potential outcomes with supply on, even while preview supply is off. Try changes positions without changing the supply. L1 and L2 identify the selected switch contacts; they are not universal on/off positions. “Both switches control the light” describes this function only. Electrical danger findings are separate and prevent a control verdict.

Keep or share a circuit

Committed edits update the page’s URL fragment without reloading the app. Copy link prepares a snapshot of the whole circuit: components and positions, core connections and identification, switch and supply states, control intent, and design inputs. Opening a circuit link restores that snapshot; later edits do not change an already copied link.

The fragment is encoded and may be compressed; it is not encrypted. Share it only with people who may see the whole plan. Copying a link does not change private Site access. Page query parameters are excluded from copied links. A local-file link requires the same HTML file at the same path on the receiving computer.

Browser storage and the URL are separate copies, with separate save statuses. You can still export a JSON plan or download the self-contained HTML app. An invalid circuit link leaves the saved or current plan intact; edit the circuit or choose Copy link to replace that fragment.

Preliminary design model

Cable capacity uses the selected 70 °C PVC copper cable table, installation method C (clipped direct) or A (in conduit in a thermally insulated wall), ambient correction and grouping correction. Each cable run has one uniform set of conditions and at most two loaded cores. The grouping count includes this cable and assumes comparable, equally loaded cables bunched together. Multiple runs of the same circuit can heat one another. Below 30 °C, this model caps the ambient factor at 1 rather than increasing the table capacity.

Voltage drop uses nominal load current, P ÷ 230 V, at power factor 1. The chosen lighting target is 3% of 230 V (6.9 V). Only the modelled circuit is included; upstream voltage drop is excluded. The result is not a prediction of actual lamp power or brightness. Parallel or ambiguous supply routes remain unassessed.

The earth-fault loop screen is restricted to a TN supply and a Type B device up to 32 A. Its selected maximum Zs is 0.95 × 230 ÷ (5 × device rating). Circuit-conductor resistance is taken at 1.2 times its 20 °C value; entered Ze is unchanged. Cable reactance and connection resistance are omitted. The screen does not simulate a trip curve or guarantee a disconnection time. TT and unknown arrangements remain unassessed.

Existing sketches keep their installation conditions unknown. A stored 5 m sketch length is excluded until it is edited or explicitly confirmed. Design examples use fictional inputs and retain a demo label when edited or exported.

What these checks mean

This prototype detects modelled connection faults and compares a limited design model with chosen thresholds. “Conditional” means the calculation is within that model’s limits. “Incomplete” means inputs or supported conditions are missing. “Issues” means a modelled threshold is exceeded.

It does not certify an installation, predict actual cable temperature or fire, or assess RCD operation, actual trip time, breaking capacity or CPC fault-energy withstand. Serious connection faults must be corrected before design calculations can be assessed.

“No modelled connection faults” and “Conditional” are not electrical safety approvals. Use this as an educational planning tool, not as instructions for work on mains wiring.
